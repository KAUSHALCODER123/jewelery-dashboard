Before you start — setting up once

You do this once, on the day you move over. Take your time with it.

Move over slowly. Do not switch off your old book yet.

This software has been checked more ways than most, but it has never yet run your shop. Only you can prove it right, and there is a calm way to do it.

  1. Set the shop up here (below), including your opening balances.
  2. For one full month — a month that has a busy day in it — keep writing every bill, purchase and receipt in whatever you use now as well as here. Yes, it is double work for a few weeks. It is the only way to be sure.
  3. At the end of the month, open Changeover Check. Type in what your old book says — cash, bank, who owes you, what you owe, gold on hand. The screen shows its own figure beside each, and the difference.
  4. Anything marked Differs is a question to settle before you trust this book, not after. The screen tells you where to look.
This is the honest bit

A blank box is shown as Not checked, never as agreement — an unanswered question is not a pass. When the figures you care about say Agrees, and still do after a second week, you can stop the double work.

Enter your shop and your opening balances

You're starting with money, gold and khatas already in hand.

Open Settings at the bottom of the side menu.

  • Company — your shop name, address, GSTIN and state. These print on every bill, so type them exactly as they should appear.
  • Accounts — open Cash Account and Bank Account and put in their opening balance: the cash in your drawer and the money in your bank on day one. This is the figure most often forgotten.
  • Customers & Suppliers — add each one, and enter what they owe you as Dr, or what you owe them as Cr. A customer who is in credit (paid you too much) goes in as Cr, not as a minus.

Don't forget the gold itself

Money is only half of it. The metal has to go in too.

What you holdWhere it goes in
Tagged pieces on the shelf Tag & Barcode — enter each batch with its weight, purity and what it cost you per gram (Cost/Gm).
Loose bullion & scrap in the safe Tag & Barcode → Made from loose metal → Opening metal. Not a purchase — you don't owe anyone for it.
Gold a karagir is holding On his record, as an opening metal balance (grams), Dr.
Metal a wholesale customer owes you On their record, as an opening metal balance in grams.
Fill in Cost/Gm on every piece

A piece with no cost recorded still shows its weight, but counts as nothing towards your stock value — so your books will say you're poorer than you are. It is the commonest reason the stock figure won't tie. Fill it in now.

2

Your day, at the counter

Open in the morning, serve through the day, count the drawer at night.

Open the shop

Sign in with your own login (never share one — see Part 5). The Dashboard greets you with today's sales, what's in stock, and anything due. A glance tells you the shop is where you left it.

A customer buys a piece and pays now

The everyday sale — cash or UPI, done and dusted.

  1. Press New Bill (or F2).
  2. Choose the customer, or leave it blank for a plain counter sale.
  3. Scan the tag. Weight, purity and rate fill in by themselves. Type the rate for the day if it isn't already set.
  4. Check the making charge on the line. Add more pieces the same way.
  5. The bill totals on the right — goods, making, GST, the lot. Put what they paid in Amount Received.
  6. Press Save, then Print.

…and they pay part now, rest later

Regular customers, known faces — the khata sale.

Same as above, but choose the customer (a credit bill needs one), tick Credit, and put only what they've handed over in Amount Received. The balance goes onto their khata automatically — you'll see it under Outstanding until it's cleared.

…and they give old gold in exchange

Trading up — an old chain towards a new one.

  1. Make the bill for the new piece as usual.
  2. Open the Old Gold section. Enter the old piece's gross weight and its tunch (purity), and the rate you're allowing.
  3. Its value comes off the bill — the customer pays only the difference.
The old gold really comes in

It isn't just knocked off the total — the metal lands in your melting pot and shows in your stock. You gave value for it, and now you hold it.

…and they pay by card

Set the payment mode to Card. If you've told the software what your bank charges for a swipe (Settings → Accounts → your bank), the customer's share of that fee is added to the bill and your own share is quietly booked as an expense — so at the end of the year you can see what taking cards actually costs you.

Why the card fee lands after GST

The fee is the bank's, not part of what you sold — so tax is worked out on the goods first, and the fee added afterward. On a credit bill where nothing is swiped yet, there's no fee until they actually pay.

A wholesale customer buys on weight, not rupees

Trade buyers who settle in metal — the weight-wise bill.

Tick Weight-wise on the bill. Now the metal isn't priced per line; instead the software works out the fine grams they owe (what they took, less any old gold they gave). On the Balance Weight tab, enter how many grams they're settling now and at what rate. Whatever's left stays owed as gold — a metal receivable on their khata, kept apart from any rupees. Making and hallmark are still charged in cash.

A scheme member comes to buy

Their gold saving plan has matured — time to spend it.

Make an ordinary bill. Once you've picked the customer, a Saving Scheme box appears with their balance. Choose it, and the balance settles the bill. Anything left over stays on the account, or you can hand it back in cash. (More on schemes in Part 3.)

Close the day

Shutters down — the two things to do before you go home.

  1. Open the Day Book. It shows the day's sales and purchases, your gold position in gross / net / fine, every account, and what came in — split by cash, card, UPI and so on.
  2. Count the drawer against the Cash Account figure. They should match.
  3. Take your backup (Part 5). Every single evening.
Only cash sits in the drawer

A card swipe, UPI, NEFT or a cheque all go to the bank, not the drawer. So if the drawer doesn't match the Cash figure, something really is short — and that's a number worth trusting.

3

The other regular jobs

Buying, making, melting, and moving money around.

Buying gold from your supplier

  1. Purchase (or F3) → choose the supplier and the metal.
  2. Cash or Credit. On Material In, enter what you received — weight, purity, rate, and any wastage % the supplier charges.
  3. The Fine + Wastage column shows what you're really paying for. Fill in GST, TCS and what you paid, then save.

On credit, it goes onto the supplier's khata as money you owe. Sending metal back to them later? Use Purchase Return, and it comes off exactly.

Sending gold to your karagir, and getting it back

The goldsmith who turns your bullion into pieces.

  1. Orders → Karagir Job Work → Issue. Enter the grams you're handing over. It sits on his metal khata — he's holding your gold now.
  2. When he returns the finished piece, press Receive. Enter what came back, the wastage you allow, and his labour rate.
  3. The software works out whether he's square, still owes you a little metal, or is owed labour in cash. His khata always shows exactly where you stand.

Melting scrap or old gold into new stock

Tag & Barcode → Made from loose metal. Make your new tagged pieces, and the fine weight comes out of your loose pool — it doesn't appear from nowhere. If you haven't enough loose metal on hand, the screen tells you so before you save.

Sending gold out to be refined

Refining → Material Out to send scrap to the refinery, Material In when the pure gold comes back. Your stock drops when it leaves and rises when it returns — the metal is never lost in between.

Paying money out — supplier, or the electricity bill

Money leaving the shop, for any reason.

Receipts & Payments → Payments. To pay a supplier, choose them and the amount. To pay an expense — electricity, tea, rent — choose the expense head instead of a party. (Make new heads under Settings → Accounts.) Either way it comes out of cash or bank and shows in your day book and your profit figure.

Why there's no separate "expense" screen

An expense is just money paid to an expense head. Keeping it a normal payment is the only way it reaches your cash book and your profit & loss — a separate list off to the side never would.

Taking money against an old bill

Receipts & Payments → Receipts. Choose the customer, enter what they've paid and how. It comes off their khata. Pick the right payment type — cash goes to the drawer, everything else to the bank.

Running a gold saving scheme

Customers who put by a little each month.

  1. Gold Scheme → Scheme Types → design the plan once. Choose how the member saves: fixed rupees, or fixed grams (so they're protected if gold goes up), and the benefit you add at the end.
  2. Members → Enrol a customer. The whole month-by-month schedule is created for you.
  3. Each month, open the member and press Receive on that instalment. On a weight plan, enter the day's rate and it shows the grams it buys.
  4. At maturity they spend it — see "A scheme member comes to buy" in Part 2. Two cards for one person? Merge Cards joins them.
Scheme money is kept separate

It's a deposit you're holding, not a sale — so it lives in its own account and never gets muddled with the customer's ordinary buying-and-selling khata.

A customer returns something

Returns → find the bill. The piece goes back into stock, the money comes off what they owe (or is refunded), and the gold and the books all move back together. Nothing is left dangling.

4

Knowing where you stand

The screens that answer "how is the shop doing?"

Who owes you, and who you owe

Outstanding lists your debtors and creditors — in money, and (for trade customers) in gold by weight. This is your morning call-list and your payment reminder in one.

One customer's full account

Ledger → Money + Gold shows a single customer's whole khata: every bill and payment, with a running balance in both rupees and fine grams, each on its own Dr/Cr — exactly as your paper khata does, but adding itself up.

What's on the shelf, and what it's worth

Stock Report. See it piece by piece, or grouped by item, and its Value at Cost. Found a piece weighed wrong at stock-take? Press Edit Stock and fix it in place — the weight is recalculated and your metal position moves with it. Sold pieces can't be edited here; fix the bill instead.

How the shop is really doing

MIS & Scheme Reports answers the owner's questions the daily screens can't:

  • Non-moving items — money sitting on a shelf untouched for months.
  • Quiet customers — regulars who've stopped coming. A call-list.
  • Top sellers and top areas — what and where actually sells.
  • Purity profit — what you sold against what it cost you.

GST time

GST Reports gives you GSTR-1, GSTR-2, the 3B summary and an HSN-wise sheet, with the CGST/SGST/IGST split done right — inter-state as IGST, within the state as CGST + SGST. Hand it to your accountant, or export it.

Every report prints and exports

On any report, Print gives you a clean sheet to file, and Export saves it as CSV, Excel or Word.

5

Keeping it safe

The habits that mean a bad day is only a bad day.

Back up every single evening

The one habit that matters more than all the others.

Settings → Data & Backup → Backup Now. Save it to a pen drive, or set up Google Drive so it happens by itself. Do it at close, every day. It takes ten seconds.

A backup on the same PC is not a backup

If the machine is stolen or its disk dies, a copy sitting on that same machine dies with it. Get it onto a pen drive you take home, or into Google Drive. That is what saves you.

If the power goes, or the PC dies

A power cut cannot corrupt your books. A half-finished bill is simply not saved — the software writes a bill completely or not at all, never half-way. When the power's back, open the software and carry on; the last saved bill is the last one you'll see.

If the machine itself dies, install the software on a new one and restore your latest backup (Settings → Data & Backup). Before it replaces anything, it copies your current books aside first — so even restoring the wrong file is undoable.

Staff logins — who can do what

Give every person their own login (Settings → Users). Never share one.

RoleCan
StaffBill, take money, enter stock, run reports.
ManagerAll of that, plus refining and removing tags.
OwnerEverything — settings, deleting bills, backups, staff logins.

So the person at the counter can sell all day, but only you can delete a bill or change a setting. Change the default password the first time you sign in.

6

When a number looks wrong

Before you worry — the usual, ordinary reasons.
What you seeUsually because
Stock value looks low Pieces with no Cost/Gm are left out of the value. The Stock Report tells you how many — fill them in.
Cash or bank is off The account's opening balance (Settings → Accounts) — the figure most forgotten on day one.
A customer's balance is wrong Their opening balance, or a bill entered in one book and not the other during changeover. Open their ledger and walk it down.
Gold on hand doesn't match the trays Loose bullion and old gold in the safe count too — not just tagged pieces.
"This tag is not in stock" That piece is already sold, melted, or at another branch. It can't be billed twice — that's the software protecting you.
A saving-scheme benefit hasn't shown It's only added once every instalment is in and the maturity date has passed. A member who stopped early gets back only what they put in.

Whatever it is, your evening backups mean you can always step back to yesterday. Nothing you do in a day is beyond undoing.

7

The gold & money rules, in one place

So no figure on a bill is ever a mystery.

Weights

Everything starts from the weight. Stones, beads and the bag it sits in aren't metal, so they come off before the piece is priced.

Net Weight = Gross − Stone − Beads − Bag Fine Weight = Net × Purity ÷ 100 Example 10.000 g gross, no stones, 91.6% pure Net = 10.000 g Fine = 10.000 × 91.6 ÷ 100 = 9.160 g pure gold

How a sale bill adds up

Goods = Net Weight × Rate per gram Making = Net Weight × Making per gram Bill = Goods + Making + Hallmark Taxable = Bill − Discounts GST = Taxable × GST% Total = Taxable + GST + Other + Card fee Balance = Total − Old Gold − Received − Scheme Example 12.000 g at ₹4,590, making ₹300/g, GST 3% Goods 12 × 4,590 = ₹55,080 Making 12 × 300 = ₹3,600 Bill = ₹58,680 GST 3% = ₹1,760.40 Total = ₹60,440.40

Old gold you take in

Fine = Net × Purity ÷ 100 Value = Fine × Rate you allow Example 3.000 g at 80%, allowed at ₹4,500 Fine = 2.400 g Value = ₹10,800 — comes off the bill

Dr and Cr, once and for all

Money khata Dr = they owe you Cr = you owe them Gold khata Dr = they owe you gold Cr = you owe them gold They are never mixed. A customer can owe you rupees AND owe you grams at the same time — two separate columns.
Balances that can't drift

Every balance here is worked out fresh from the bills and receipts, not kept as a running tally. So editing or deleting anything corrects itself — there's nothing to unwind by hand, and no way for a figure to quietly go wrong.
