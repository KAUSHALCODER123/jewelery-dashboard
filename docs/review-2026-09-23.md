# Parivar Jewellery ERP — a shop owner's review

**Reviewed:** v1.26.5, branch `v1.26.0-old-gold-untagged-tour`, 23 September 2026
**Reviewer persona:** 55-year-old owner of a third-generation family jewellery shop in Pune. Gold, silver, a little diamond. Cash and UPI. Three sales staff and one karigar on the payroll. Has run the shop on a register, then Excel, then a Tally-style package, and has thrown two ERP vendors out of the shop.
**Format:** the owner talks. Under most points there is a short *Dev note* in plain type that names the cause and the file, so the rant can go straight onto the backlog.

**One correction before we start.** The brief said this app is Tauri with Drizzle. It is not. It is Electron with better-sqlite3 and hand-written SQL. Nothing below depends on that, but the reader comparing against the brief should not think I opened the wrong repo.

## How this was tested

- Read the code in `electron/` and `src/`, and drove the real API and the built renderer against **throwaway databases in a temp folder**. The real database on this machine was never opened. The `npm run demo` seeder, which rewrites the live database, was never run.
- Two data passes: a fresh database for the first-day experience, then the shared demo shop (`test/demo-data.cjs`) plus the tour extras.
- Executed hands-on: old gold on a sale bill with making negotiated, a B2B GST bill and an inter-state bill, a credit (udhaar) sale with part payment and a later receipt, karigar issue and receive, gold-scheme enrolment and redemption, an exchange bigger than the purchase, a partial return of a two-line bill, two rates on one bill, backdated and future-dated bills, duplicate HUID, staff permissions, and a scale run of 3,000 tags and 600 bills.
- Judged from code and a rendered print only: the A4 invoice, tag labels, Google Drive backup, WhatsApp share. **No physical printer was used.** The phone view was exercised on localhost.
- Screens in `docs/review/` are captures from this run.

---

## 1. Quick verdict

**With reservations. Not today, and not with my staff on it unsupervised.**

The bones are better than anything a vendor has shown me. It knows what a khata is. It knows old gold, karigar, saving schemes, weightwise settlement, and it produces a GSTR-1 that looks like a GSTR-1. It works with the internet off. The person who wrote it has stood behind a jewellery counter, or listened carefully to someone who has.

But I found four ways to make money disappear from the books or to let a salesman quietly rewrite yesterday, and none of them needed any cleverness. A counter bill saved with the "received" box left blank loses the whole amount from the accounts. A staff login can edit or delete any saved bill and any return with no trace. The same tag can be "returned" twice and the customer credited twice. And nothing stops a bill being dated 2020 or 2030. Until those are closed, this is a very good calculator, not a book of accounts I would sign a GST return from.

Fix the dealbreakers and I would run it in parallel with my old books for a month, which, to its credit, the software itself tells me to do.

## 2. Dealbreakers

*Definition used: anything that could cause financial loss, a legal penalty, or unrecoverable data loss within the first month.*

### D1. A counter bill with "Amount Received" left blank makes the money vanish

I made two counter bills, no customer selected, five-gram rings, ₹35,535 each. On the first I left "Amount Received" blank, the way a busy salesman will. On the second I typed the amount.

| Report | What it showed |
|---|---|
| Day Book, sales in cash | ₹71,070 (both bills) |
| Cash Book, money in | ₹35,535 (one bill) |
| Receivable / Outstanding | ₹0, nobody |
| Trial balance | out by ₹35,535, parked under "Capital / Opening Difference" |

So the day book says I sold ₹71,070 for cash, the cash drawer says ₹35,535 came in, and nobody owes me the rest. At the end of the day the two figures will not match and the software cannot tell me why. In a shop doing thirty counter bills a day this will happen by Tuesday. The printed bill even says "Cash Received: 0.00, Balance: ₹35,535" and lets me hand it to the customer.

*Dev note.* `sale.save` in `electron/api.cjs` posts the unpaid balance to the party's khata. With `party_id` null there is no khata, and the balance leg is simply not posted, so debits no longer equal credits. The renderer's only guard is `SalesInvoice.tsx:378`, which insists on a customer for a **credit** bill; a cash bill with a short or blank receipt passes. Either default `amount_received` to the payable amount for a walk-in, or refuse to save a walk-in bill with a balance. The trial balance's "Capital / Opening Difference" line (see `docs/review/trial-balance-capital-difference.png`) is where the hole shows up.

### D2. Staff can rewrite or delete history with no trail

A staff login is meant for "billing, receipts, orders, stock entry". Through the IPC layer a staff login can also: re-save any saved bill (change the date, the customer, the discount, the items), delete sales returns and purchase returns, delete karigar issue and receive slips, delete stock settlements and transfers, delete branches, edit tags in bulk, and change application settings. Only outright deletion of bills, purchases, vouchers, orders and masters is held back for the owner; editing them is not.

There is no audit table. The `sale` row carries `created_at` and nothing else: no who, no when-changed, no before-and-after. An owner deleting a bill removes it, its ledger legs, and its number, leaving a gap in the series and no record that COM5 ever existed.

For a shop where the owner is not at the counter all day, this is the one that keeps me on my old software. The old software is worse in every way except that it does not let the boy on the counter re-date a bill.

*Dev note.* `CHANNEL_PERMISSION` in `electron/main.cjs` gates only a short list; everything else is "daily". Missing from the gate: `sale:save` on an existing id, `saleReturn:remove`, `purchaseReturn:remove`, `karagir:removeIssue`, `karagir:removeReceive`, `stockSettlement:remove`, `stockTransfer:remove`, `branch:remove`, `tagStock:updateRows`, `settings:set`. Needed: an `audit_log` table written from every save/remove with user, timestamp, doc, and old/new JSON; a per-role rule "staff may edit a bill only on the day it was made"; and soft delete (a `cancelled` flag that keeps the number) instead of `DELETE FROM sale`.

### D3. The same piece can be returned twice and the customer paid twice

Sold a two-line bill. Returned line one. Returned line one *again* against the same bill. Both returns saved. The tag went back into stock once; the customer's khata was credited twice. Then I returned line two at ₹9,000 a gram when it was sold at ₹6,500, and that saved too. A friendly salesman and a friendly customer can drain the till through the Returns screen.

*Dev note.* `saleReturn.save` (`electron/api.cjs` around line 3433) checks that a tag exists but not that it is currently SOLD on the bill named in `against_sale_id`, nor that the return rate and weight match the sold line, nor that the line has not already been returned. Also: editing the original bill to drop the returned line succeeds and leaves the returns pointing at a bill that no longer contains that item; and deleting a bill that has a return surfaces the raw SQLite text "FOREIGN KEY constraint failed" to the user.

### D4. Any date goes, forever

I saved a bill dated 1 January 2020 and another dated 1 January 2030. Both saved, numbered COM19 and COM20 in sequence, so the register now has a 2020 bill with a higher number than a 2026 bill. I then changed the date of a saved bill from September to January. Saved. No period lock, no financial-year boundary on numbering, no "this month is closed".

A GST officer reading a register where bill numbers and dates do not run together will not accept "the software allowed it". Neither will my CA.

*Dev note.* `sale.save` takes `bill_date` as given (`electron/api.cjs` ~1768). `voucher_series` has one running `next_no` with no FY component, although `company` stores `fy_start`/`fy_end`. Needed: a closed-period date in settings (default: yesterday for staff, open for owner), a warning when a date is outside the current FY, and per-FY numbering with a prefix such as `COM/26-27/0001`.

## 3. Feature by feature

### 3.1 Making a bill

**What it does.** One screen (`docs/review/sales-invoice-screen.png`). Scan or type a tag and the weights fill in. Columns for gross, purity, stone, net, rate per 10 g, making as per-gram or percent, line total. Old gold lines underneath. Payment can be split across cash, UPI, card, cheque, and the split must foot or it refuses to save. Making can be negotiated three ways, and there is a "Fit Making" button that back-solves the making so the total lands on the round figure the customer and I shook hands on. That last one is the first thing in any ERP I have seen that understands how a bill is actually closed at a counter.

**Why is it done this way?** The rate is typed on every bill. There is no rate board. The screen does not know today's 22K rate, so the salesman types 65000 into a 10 g rate box on every bill. The rate changes two or three times a day here. A wrong zero is a wrong bill. I typed a rate of ₹650 per 10 g and it saved. I typed a rate of zero and it saved. I gave a 95% bill discount and it saved. Nothing is checked against anything, because there is nothing to check against.

*Dev note.* The schema has a `metal_rate` table (one row per group per day) and **nothing in the code reads or writes it**. `rate_master` handles making and wastage defaults only. Needed: a rate board (group → rate, timestamped, with history), the bill defaulting to it, a tolerance warning when a line rate is more than a few percent off the board, a discount ceiling per role, and "rate on this bill differs from the board rate" printed on the bill footer for audit.

**Real scenario, old gold exchange.** Customer brings a bangle, 8.2 g gross, 7.9 g net. We agree to take it at 88 touch. Rate ₹6,500. New piece 17.2 g at 25% off making. The bill did exactly what I would do on paper: valued the old gold at 6.952 g fine, ₹45,188, GST charged on the full sale value not the net of exchange (correct, that is the law), old gold netted off at the bottom. The bangle went into "URD loose stock" as fine weight so I can see what is in the melting tin. Good.

But: the old-gold line has no wastage or ghat field. The only lever is purity. Every shop I know deducts "2% ghat" as a separate line the customer can see; folding it into the purity number is how you get an argument. The standalone Old Gold Purchase bill has a single rupee "Deduction" box, which is the same problem in a different coat. I also typed purity 120 and net weight greater than gross on an old-gold line; both saved.

*Dev note.* `sale_urd` and `urd_bill` lines: add `deduction_pct` (wastage/ghat) alongside purity so the printed line reads "7.900 g × 88% less 2%". Validate purity 0–100 and net ≤ gross in `calc.urdLine` / `urd.save` (~2192).

**Real scenario, exchange bigger than the purchase.** 30 g of old gold against a small ring. The bill went negative by ₹1.5 lakh, was marked "Settled", and the customer's khata now shows ₹1.5 lakh credit (I owe them). No cash went out. That is a reasonable answer, but the bill's "Cash" mode and "Settled" label tell the counter nothing about a customer who is walking out owed ₹1.5 lakh. It should say "Pay out ₹1,50,145" and post the payout, or refuse without a payment leg.

### 3.2 GST and printing

**What it does.** 3% default, HSN 7113 on the item, CGST/SGST when the party's state is mine and IGST when it is not, B2B when the party has a GSTIN and B2C otherwise. GSTR-1, GSTR-2, GSTR-3B, HSN summary, and a TCS/TDS list. An estimate series with GST off. The A4 invoice prints HSN, purity, HUID, rate per 10 g, amount in words, the declaration, bank details, and the customer's pending balance. This is complete enough that I believe it.

**What is questionable.**

- Making is taxed at 3% with the metal. That is what everyone does and what my CA accepts, but a "separate making at 5%" switch exists in some shops' practice; not a blocker.
- No e-invoice / IRN. If turnover crosses the threshold, B2B bills need an IRN and QR. Nothing here.
- The invoice prints the customer's total pending balance in the footer. Some customers will object to a ₹95,000 "Pending Balance" printed on the bill they take home to show their family. Make it optional.
- GSTIN is a free text box. Nothing checks the 15-character format or that its first two digits match the state I chose. A wrong GSTIN on a B2B invoice costs my customer their input credit and costs me a phone call.
- Deleting a bill leaves a gap in the series. GSTR-1 asks for cancelled numbers. There is no cancelled state to report.

*Dev note.* `reports.gstReturn` and friends in `electron/api.cjs` (from ~4371) are sound. Add GSTIN checksum validation in `party.save`, a `cancelled` flag on `sale` (see D2), and an optional footer setting for the pending balance in `src/print/invoice.ts`.

### 3.3 Udhaar, receipts, outstanding

**What it does.** Mark a bill credit, take part payment, the rest lands on the khata. Receipts later, cash or bank. Outstanding list by money and, separately, by metal. A Ledger screen in Dr/Cr, money or grams or both. This matches how a khata is kept and I have no complaint with the arithmetic: I traced one customer through sale, part payment, two receipts, and the closing balance was right every time.

**What is questionable.**

- A receipt is against a party, not a bill. Fine for a small shop, but when Ramesh has three open bills and pays ₹10,000, nobody can later say which bill it cleared. No ageing (30/60/90 days) either.
- An over-receipt of ₹1 lakh against a ₹5 lakh balance was accepted with no prompt. Usually right (it is an advance), but silently.
- A credit bill needs a customer in the screen, but the API accepts one without. Any future screen or the phone view that calls the API directly inherits D1.
- The "Cash / Credit" toggle and the "Amount Received" box are independent. A bill can be "Cash" with a balance. Pick one meaning.

### 3.4 Karigar (job work)

**What it does.** Issue metal to a karigar with purity and allowed wastage, receive the piece back with wastage and labour, the ledger shows issued, received, wastage and the shortfall he cannot account for, labour with TDS. Orders link to the karigar and there is a late-order report. This is the part a Tally-type package never has, and it is done properly. The 25 g issue, 24.1 g back with 3% allowed, came out to a 0.162 g shortfall on his account, which is what my notebook would say.

**What is questionable.**

- I issued 500 g when the safe held 45 g. Accepted. The loose stock report now says minus 452 g of gold. A negative safe is a data entry error every time; refuse it.
- The received piece does not become a tag. It lands as loose fine weight and then I have to go to Tag & Barcode and make the piece again by hand, retyping the weight I just typed. One button, "tag this piece", would save the retyping and the mistakes.
- Karigar cannot see his own ledger. A printable karigar statement for the month would end a lot of arguments.

*Dev note.* `karagir.issue` (~3145) does not compare against `looseStock.summary().available_fine`. Receive → `tagStock.saveBatch` handoff is a UI feature, the API pieces exist.

### 3.5 Gold saving scheme

**What it does.** Scheme types on amount, on weight, and on making discount. Enrol a member, instalments are generated, receipts book to a "Gold Saving Scheme" liability account, the balance can be redeemed on a bill and the software will not let the same balance be redeemed twice (I tried). Members list with overdue count. Good.

**What is questionable.**

- Early redemption at full value. A member who paid 3 of 11 could redeem ₹6,000 today with no forfeit or penalty. Every 11+1 scheme has terms; the software has none.
- The 11+1 bonus did not appear when 11 of 11 were paid, apparently because the maturity date had not arrived. Probably intended, but the member screen does not say "bonus ₹2,000 on 1 Oct".
- No printed passbook, no receipt series printed for the customer. Scheme customers keep a card; give them one.

### 3.6 Returns

Covered under D3. Beyond the bug: a return with "refund amount 0" posts a credit note on the khata, and a cash refund goes out through the cash book, both correct. The screen copies the sold line's rate only if you choose the line from the bill; a hand-typed line is accepted at any rate. A return should never be typed by hand against a numbered bill.

### 3.7 Gold rate

There isn't one. See 3.1. This is the most surprising gap in the product. Stock is valued at cost, which is right for the books, but the owner also needs "what is my stock worth at today's bhav", and the Changeover screen already knows that 2,436 pieces have no cost recorded so it cannot value them at all.

### 3.8 Tags, HUID, labels

**What it does.** Batch tag entry, barcodes, TSC 100×15 rat-tail tags with the shop mark on the fold-over, a 50×12 roll, and A4 sheets. Reprint tracking. Stock verification screen for a physical count. Untagged metal can be sold "from purchase" with a guard against drawing more than the invoice holds.

**What is questionable.**

- HUID is a free text box. I entered the same HUID on two tags: accepted. A three-character lowercase HUID: accepted. A tag with zero weight, a tag with 150% purity, a tag with stone weight larger than gross: all accepted. BIS now asks for the HUID on every hallmarked piece and on the invoice; a duplicate is a compliance problem and a stock-count nightmare.
- HUID is printed on the invoice, good, but not on the tag label. The label is where I need it when the customer asks.
- I could not test the physical label; judged from the layout code only.

*Dev note.* Add `UNIQUE(huid) WHERE huid <> ''` on `tag_stock` and validate the 6-character alphanumeric format in `tagStock.saveBatch` and `updateRows`. Range checks on weight/purity/stone in the same place. Add HUID to the TSC head layout in `src/print/barcode.ts` if it fits the 50 mm head.

### 3.9 Stock, day book, end of day, changeover

**Day Book** shows sales and purchases split cash/credit, received by mode, cash and bank opening/closing. **Changeover Check** (`docs/review/changeover-check.png`) puts the software's eight key balances beside a box for "what your old books say" and marks each agree/differ. That screen is the most honest thing an ERP vendor has ever put in front of me and it is the reason my verdict is "with reservations" and not "no".

**What is missing** is the thing I do every night: count the drawer. There is no end-of-day close where I type "counted cash ₹1,28,500, UPI as per phone ₹42,000", see the difference against the book, write a reason, and lock the day. The Changeover screen is close to that but it is framed as a one-time migration tool, not a nightly ritual, and it locks nothing.

Dashboard "Today's Sales" counts bills whether or not anything was received. Put "received today" next to it or it flatters me.

### 3.10 Users, permissions, fraud

Covered under D2. Also:

- Default login admin / admin. A yellow banner says change it (`docs/review/users-default-password.png`). Nothing forces it. Password minimum is four characters. The first run should not let me bill until the owner password is changed.
- Three roles is enough. But the "What Each Role Can Do" table on the Users tab describes intent, not what the IPC layer enforces (see D2).
- No discount ceiling, no rate tolerance, no "manager approval" step on anything.

### 3.11 Offline, backup, phone

**Offline:** fully local SQLite, no network needed for anything I did. Power cut mid-bill loses the unsaved bill and nothing else; each save is one transaction. This is right.

**Backup:** manual copy to a file the owner picks, restore keeps a safety copy of the live file first. Google Drive backup with a daily-on-startup option, using a scope that can only see its own files and storing the token encrypted. Sensible design; I read it, I did not run it. There is no local automatic backup (a nightly copy to a second folder or pen drive). Shops without a Google account get nothing automatic.

**Phone view:** read-only, own login with the desktop's users, a disabled user loses the phone too, wrong-password attempts are throttled (I got locked after twelve). Any write returns "Read only". Off by default. This is exactly as much phone as I want.

### 3.12 Orders, refining, loyalty, branches, gold loan

- **Orders** take an advance, link to a karigar, and convert to a bill without counting the advance twice. The late-orders report is useful before a wedding season. Nothing to complain about.
- **Refining** books scrap out and pure metal back with burn loss. Fine.
- **Loyalty points** as a percent of goods for flagged customers. Nobody in my trade runs points; they run schemes. Harmless.
- **Branches & Transfer** is a whole menu item for a single-shop owner. It reads as "this vendor is chasing bigger shops than me". Hide it unless a second branch exists.
- **Gold loan / EMI** is not in the product. Correct call: that is lending, with RBI implications, and most shops run it off-book or through an NBFC partner. Say so in the manual so the omission looks deliberate.

### 3.13 Speed

3,000 tags loaded in two seconds, 600 bills in half a second, every report under 70 ms with that data. The Sales Register caps at 500 rows, which a busy shop crosses in a fortnight; add paging or a "load more". No speed complaints otherwise.

## Appendix B. What was not tested

- Physical printing on the TSC TL240 or an A4 printer. The invoice was rendered to HTML and read; the label layout was read from code.
- Google Drive OAuth, upload, and restore from Drive. Read only.
- WhatsApp, SMS, email sends. Stubbed.
- Multi-branch transfers beyond a glance at the screen.
- A real shop's database. Only demo and generated data.
