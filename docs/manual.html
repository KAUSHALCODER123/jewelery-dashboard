1. Before You Begin

What this software does

Parivar Jewellery ERP runs a retail jewellery shop end to end. It keeps your stock piece by piece, makes GST tax invoices, tracks what every customer owes you in both rupees and gold, records purchases from suppliers, follows orders given to karagirs, handles metal sent for refining, and runs monthly gold saving schemes.

It works without internet

Everything runs on your own computer. No internet connection is needed to make a bill, and your customer and stock information never leaves the machine. The only time the software opens the internet is when you press the WhatsApp or Email button, and even then it just opens WhatsApp or your mail program with the message ready — you still press send yourself.

Installing

  1. Run the installer and choose where to install.
  2. Open Parivar Jewellery ERP from the Start menu or desktop shortcut.
  3. The first time it opens, the software creates an empty database and fills in standard item groups (22K, 18K and so on) and standard accounts for you.

Signing in the first time

The software asks for a username and password every time it opens. On a brand-new installation there is one account ready for you:

Username: admin Password: admin
Change this password before you start billing Until you do, anyone who has heard of this software can open your books. Sign in, go to Settings → Users, and change it. The software keeps warning you on that screen until you have.

Where your data is kept

All your data sits in a single file on this computer. You can see its exact location at any time under Settings → Data & Backup.

Your data is only on this computer There is no copy anywhere else. If this computer is lost, stolen or its hard disk fails, your books are gone with it. Take a backup at the end of every working day — see section 16. It takes ten seconds.

The order to set things up

Each step below needs the one before it, so follow this order the first time:

2. Signing In & Getting Around

Signing in

Type your username and password and press Sign In. If either is wrong you are told only "Invalid username or password" — the software never reveals which part was wrong, so nobody can fish for valid usernames.

A login the owner has disabled cannot sign in at all, even with the correct password.

Signing out

Your name sits at the top right. Click it and choose Sign out. Do this whenever you leave the counter — the next person signs in with their own login, so the shop knows who did what.

The search box — the fastest way to work

Press Ctrl + K anywhere in the software. A search box opens in the middle of the screen. Start typing and it will find:

  • Any screen — type stock and press Enter to open the Stock Report.
  • Any customer or supplier — type a name and press Enter to open their khata directly.

Use the ↑ and ↓ keys to choose, Enter to open, Esc to close.

Light and dark appearance

The moon button switches to a dark screen, which is easier on the eyes in a dim shop or at night. Your choice is remembered the next time you open the software.

Things that behave the same everywhere

  • Grids grow by themselves. Whenever you start typing in the last empty row of a table, a fresh blank row appears below. You never need to press an "add row" button.
  • Grey boxes are calculated. If a box has a grey background you cannot type in it — the software works it out for you (net weight, fine weight, amounts, totals).
  • Delete a row by moving your mouse over it and clicking the small ✕ that appears on the far left.
  • Messages appear briefly at the bottom right — green tick for success, red for a problem. Read the red ones; they explain exactly what is wrong.

2b. Before You Trust This Software

Read this before you move your shop onto it. It is the most important page here.

Run both systems side by side first For at least a week — a month is better, and a month that includes a busy day is better still — keep entering every bill, purchase and receipt in whatever you use now as well as here. Yes, it is double work. It is the only way to find out whether the two agree before your books depend on the answer.
  1. Set the shop up (section 3) and enter your opening balances: cash in hand, bank, what each customer owes you, what you owe each supplier, and your stock. If a customer is in credit, enter the amount as Cr rather than as a minus.
  2. Enter the metal too, which is easy to forget. Tagged pieces go in on the tag grid; loose bullion and scrap in the safe go in under Tag & Barcode → Made from loose metal → Opening metal. Do not enter it as a purchase — you do not owe anyone for it. If a goldsmith is holding your gold, put that on his record as an opening metal balance.
  3. Work normally for a week or a month, entering everything in both systems.
  4. Open Changeover Check. Set the date to the last day you entered.
  5. Type what your old system says for each figure — cash, bank, customers owing, suppliers owed, gold on hand, stock value, GST, scheme deposits.
  6. Anything marked Differs is a question to answer before you switch, not after. The screen tells you where to look, and lists every customer and supplier so you can find which one is out.

A box you leave blank is reported as Not checked — never as agreement. An empty form does not mean everything is fine; it means you have not looked yet.

The usual reasons a figure will not tie

Stock value is lowPieces with no Cost/Gm are left out of the valuation entirely. The screen tells you how many. Fill them in on the Stock Report.
Cash or bank is outCheck the account's opening balance under Settings → Accounts. It is the figure most often forgotten on day one.
A customer is outTheir opening balance, or a bill entered in one system and not the other. The lists at the bottom of the screen show every party.
Gold on hand is outCount the trays. Remember loose metal and old gold in the safe count too, not just tagged pieces.

When everything you care about says Agrees, and it still does after a second week, you can stop the double entry.

A power cut cannot corrupt your books If the power drops mid-bill, or someone pulls the plug, the half-finished bill is simply not saved — the software either writes a document completely or not at all, never half-way. When the power comes back, open the software and carry on; the last saved bill is the last one you will see. Take your daily backup anyway (section 16): it protects against a failed disk, which no software can.
Numbers are never reused Once a bill number is given out it is gone forever, even if you delete that bill. This is deliberate — a missing number in your books is far safer than the same number appearing on two different bills.

Accounts tab

The list of accounts the software posts to — Cash, Bank, Sales, Purchase, GST Payable, Old Gold Purchase, Gold Saving Scheme and Shop Expenses. These are created for you and marked "System". You do not need to change anything here.

Taking cards

Open your bank account from the list and tick Card swap account. Enter what the bank charges, split two ways: For Customer in % is added to their bill, For Us in % is what you absorb.

On a bill paid by Card, the customer's share appears as Card Charges in the totals, and your share is recorded against a Card Charges expense head so you can see what taking cards actually costs you over a year.

Card charges are added after GST The fee is the bank's, not part of what you sold — so tax is worked out on the goods first and the fee is added afterwards. Only one account can be your card account.

The fee is worked out on what actually went through the machine. On a credit bill where the customer has not paid yet, no card has been presented, so there is no fee — it appears when they pay.

Making & Wastage tab — rates you charge every day

Most shops charge the same making on the same kind of item, day after day. Set it once here and it fills itself in from then on.

  1. Choose Making or Wastage at the top.
  2. Press New Rule.
  3. Pick a whole item group (for example all 22K Gold) or one item (for example only Bangles).
  4. Type the rate and press Save.
Making per gramCharged on the net weight, the usual way
Flat charge per pieceFor items you charge a fixed making on, whatever they weigh
Wastage %Your usual wastage on a purchase from a supplier

An item's own rule beats its group's. Set 22K Gold to ₹400 and Bangles to ₹550, and bangles are charged ₹550 while every other 22K item is charged ₹400. Where a number came from is shown under the box, so nothing appears from nowhere.

The rates fill themselves into three places:

  • the Making /gm box when you tag a new batch of stock;
  • the making on a bill line, if that piece was tagged without one;
  • the Wastage % on a purchase line.
These only start you off A rate that has filled itself in is just a starting point — change it on any row, any time, and what you leave there is what is saved. The software will never go back and change a bill or a tag you have already saved. That matters most when the right answer is nothing: if you gave making free on a bill, it stays free.

4. Creating Items

Press F6 or click Item Creation.

What an "item" means here

An item is a kind of ornament, not a single piece. "Ring" is one item, even though you may have forty rings in the shop. The individual rings are entered later as tags (section 5).

Item Types and Item Groups

Before creating items, understand these two lists:

  • Item Type — the metal: Gold, Silver, Platinum, Diamond, Stone.
  • Item Group — the quality within that metal, and this is what sets the purity: 22K Gold is 91.6%, 18K Gold is 75%, Silver is 92.5%, and so on.

Both lists are ready to use. To add your own, press the Item Groups or Item Types buttons at the top of the screen. When adding a group, type its name, its purity percentage, and pick which metal it belongs to.

Creating an item

  1. Press New Item.
  2. Item Name — for example Ring.
  3. Item Type — Gold.
  4. Item Group — 22K Gold. The purity shown beside each group is what will be filled in automatically when you enter stock.
  5. Design — optional, for example Antique or Filigree.
  6. Weight / Qty — see the explanation below.
  7. UOM — the unit: GRAM, CARAT, PCS or TOLA.
  8. HSN Code — the GST code printed on the bill. 7113 is the usual code for gold jewellery.
  9. Reorder Level — optional. Set the smallest number of pieces of this item you want to keep. When stock drops below it, a red Reorder alert appears at the top of the Stock Report telling you how many are short. Leave it 0 to switch the alert off.
  10. Press Save.

Weight-wise or Quantity-wise?

ChoiceUse it forHow it is priced
Weight-wise Almost everything — rings, chains, bangles, necklaces Net weight × rate per gram
Quantity-wise Things sold by the piece — coins, fixed-price articles Number of pieces × rate

The tag prefix

As you type the item name, the software shows the tag prefix it will use — the first three letters in capitals. Ring becomes RIN, so its pieces will be tagged RIN00001, RIN00002 and so on. Necklace becomes NEC.

Deleting items Once an item has stock tags against it, the software will not let you delete it — that would leave your stock records pointing at nothing. Delete the tags first if you genuinely need to remove the item.

5. Entering Your Stock (Tag & Barcode)

Press F7 or click Tag & Barcode. This is where every physical piece in your shop is entered and given its own tag number.

Entering pieces

  1. Choose the item from Select Item — for example Ring. The prefix and default purity for that item appear beside it.
  2. A grid appears. One row is one physical piece.
  3. Fill in the weights across the row. A new blank row appears as soon as you start typing.
  4. Check the totals under the grid, then press Save … tags.

What each column means

Gross WtThe full weight of the piece as it comes off the scale, including stones
Black B.Weight of black beads, if any
Stone WtWeight of stones set in the piece
Stone RatePrice per unit of stone weight. The stone value is charged on top of the metal on the bill. Leave blank for a plain gold piece.
Dia. WtDiamond weight. Like stones, it comes out of the metal — diamonds are not gold — and is priced separately.
Dia. RatePrice per unit of diamond weight.
Purity %Filled in from the item group (91.6 for 22K). Change it for an odd piece.
Mkg/GmYour making charge per gram for this piece. Filled into the bill automatically later.
HallmarkHallmarking charge for this piece
Cost/GmWhat you paid, per gram of fine metal. Used for the stock valuation on the Stock Report. Never printed on a customer's bill — leave it blank if you would rather not record it.
QtyOnly for quantity-wise items such as coins
Net Wt autoGross − Stone − Black beads − Diamond. The actual metal.
Fine Wt autoNet × Purity ÷ 100. The pure gold content.
HUIDThe six-character hallmark ID, if the piece has one
Example — a 22K ring Gross Wt 10.000 g Stone Wt 0.000 g Net Wt 10.000 g (10.000 − 0.000) Purity 91.6 % Fine Wt 9.160 g (10.000 × 91.6 ÷ 100)

Tag numbers

You do not type tag numbers. When you save, the software gives each piece the next free number for that item — RIN00001, RIN00002 and so on. Write this number on the physical tag you tie to the piece, or print a barcode for it.

The totals line

Under the grid you see Gross Wt, Net Wt, Fine Wt and Pieces for everything you are entering. Check these against your physical weighing before saving — it is the easiest moment to catch a typing mistake.

The stock list below

Everything already tagged is listed underneath. You can search by tag or item name, and filter by In stock, Sold or All. Export saves the list as a CSV file you can open in Excel.

You cannot delete a sold tag Once a piece has been billed, its tag is part of that bill's history. If the tag was entered wrongly, correct or delete the bill — the piece then returns to stock and can be deleted or re-entered.

Tagging many pieces at once

Entering forty bangles one row at a time is slow. There are four faster ways.

1. Set what is the same for every piece

Above the grid is a Same for every piece row — purity, making per gram, hallmark charge, location, and Category (Ladies/Gents), Salesman, Shelf / Tray and Size. Anything you set there is stamped onto every piece in the batch. Press Apply to all rows to push it onto rows you have already typed. Later, the Stock Report can group your stock by Category, Salesman or Shelf / Tray, so you can see what is where.

If you have set up a Making Master (section 3), the making per gram is already filled in when you choose the item, and a note under the box tells you which rule it came from. Change it whenever a batch is different — the master only starts you off.

Bag Weight comes off the metal If you weigh a piece in its pouch, put the pouch's weight in Bag Wt. It is taken off before the piece is priced, exactly like stones and beads are — so the customer is never charged a gold rate for plastic.

Which labels still need printing

The tagged-stock list has a Not printed only filter. Every piece shows Not printed until its label has actually come out of the printer, so you can work through a big batch over several sittings without labelling anything twice. A piece is only counted once you press Print — cancel the dialog and it stays on the list.

2. Duplicate a row

Type one piece, then press the ×10 button at the end of its row to make ten more copies of it. Change the number beside Add rows to duplicate a different amount. Every copy still gets its own tag number when you save.

Use this for batches of identical items — coins, plain bangles, standard chains.

3. Paste from Excel

Weigh everything into a spreadsheet, copy the block of cells, and paste anywhere in the grid. One line becomes one piece. The columns must be in this order:

Gross Wt, Black Beads, Stone Wt, Purity, Making/gm, Hallmark, Cost/Gm, Qty, HUID, Location, Stone Rate, Dia. Wt, Dia. Rate

Only the first column is required — leave the rest blank to use your defaults. The stone and diamond rate columns are at the end, so a spreadsheet made before they existed still lines up.

If your file has a header row, the columns are matched by name, so the order does not matter and an older file that has no Cost/Gm column still loads correctly. Only a headerless paste relies on the exact order above.

4. Import a CSV file

  1. Press Import CSV above the grid.
  2. Press Download template to get a correctly-shaped file, or paste your rows straight into the box.
  3. Check the preview — it shows the net and fine weight worked out for every row before anything is created.
  4. Press Load rows. They appear in the grid.
  5. Check the totals against your physical weighing, then press Save.
Nothing is created until you press Save Importing only fills the grid. You always get to look at the rows and the totals first, and you can still edit or delete any of them.

Printing barcode labels

In the Tagged Stock list, tick the pieces you want (or the box in the header to take all of them) and press Print labels.

Label sizeJewellery tag roll (50 × 12 mm), A4 sheet of 65, or A4 sheet of 24
Copies eachTwo labels per piece if you tag both ends of a dumbbell tag
Skip labelsStart part-way down a sheet you have already used some of
Show on the labelItem name, purity, gross weight, net weight — tick what fits

The preview shows exactly what will print. The barcode is standard Code 128, which every ordinary barcode scanner reads without any setup — scan it straight into the Tag box on a sales bill.

Check the alignment first Print one sheet on plain paper and hold it against your label sheet before running the real ones.

Loose metal and tagged pieces

Your gold sits in the shop in two forms, and the software keeps them apart.

FormWhat it is
Tagged pieces Finished ornaments, each with its own tag number, ready to sell
Loose metal Bars, scrap, metal back from the refiner, and old gold bought from customers — no tags, just weight

Your total stock is both added together. The Day Book shows the split, so you can see at a glance how much is on the shelf and how much is still waiting to be made up.

Turning loose metal into pieces

When the karagir turns your bar into bangles, that weight must move from loose to tagged — not be added again. Otherwise buying 100 g and then tagging 100 g of bangles would look like 200 g of gold, and your books would be wrong.

  1. Open Tag & Barcode.
  2. At the top, switch Where is this metal from? to From loose metal.
  3. A band appears showing how much loose metal you have and how much these pieces will use.
  4. Enter the pieces exactly as normal — the same grid, duplicate, paste and import all still work.
  5. Press Convert.

The tags are created and the same fine weight comes out of your loose stock. Your total gold does not change — it has only changed form.

Before loose 50.000 g tagged 0.000 g total 50.000 g Convert 40 g of loose metal into one kada After loose 10.000 g tagged 40.000 g total 50.000 g
The software will not let you use metal you do not have If the pieces need more fine weight than is loose, the band turns red, it tells you how much you are short, and Convert is blocked. Either the weights are wrong, or a purchase has not been entered yet.
Which mode should I use? New stock when you are first entering what is already in the shop, or for pieces bought ready-made as finished ornaments. From loose metal whenever the pieces were made from bar or scrap you already own.

Working on many tags at once

The tick boxes in the stock list also let you Export just those rows, or Delete them together. Pieces that have been sold or melted are skipped — the software tells you how many it could not remove.

Starting with a shop full of stock Take one item at a time — all your rings, then all your chains. Weigh a batch into a spreadsheet, import it, check the totals, save, then print and tie the labels on. Slow the first time, quick forever afterwards.

6. Customers and Suppliers

Press F8 for Customers. Suppliers is just below it and works identically.

Do I have to add every customer?

No. For a walk-in cash sale you can simply type a name on the bill and leave it at that. Add a customer properly when you want to:

  • give credit and track what they owe you,
  • keep a khata of their old gold and past purchases,
  • send them bills and reminders on WhatsApp,
  • enrol them in a gold saving scheme.

Adding a customer

Press New Customer. The form is in five parts.

Identity

Name (required), Mobile, WhatsApp No, Email, Reference Name. The WhatsApp number is used when you send bills and reminders — if it is the same as the mobile, type it in both.

Address

Area, City, Taluka, District, State and full address. Area is worth filling in — reports can group your customers by area.

KYC & Dates

Aadhaar, PAN and GSTIN for larger transactions. Birth date and anniversary let you greet customers — a small thing that brings people back.

Opening Balance — money

This is where you enter what a customer already owed you before you started using this software.

SideMeaningExample
Debit (Dr)They owe youCustomer took goods worth ₹9,500 on credit
Credit (Cr)You owe themCustomer paid ₹2,400 advance
Enter opening balances once, carefully Get these right at the start. If you change an opening balance later, every balance and khata for that person shifts by the difference.

Opening Metal Balance — gold

Separately from money, a customer or supplier can owe you metal. If a supplier is holding 50 grams of your fine gold, enter 50 against Gold on the Debit side. This feeds the gold khata described in section 14.

The customer list

Shows name, mobile, area, city, GSTIN and current balance. Balances are colour-coded: red Dr means they owe you, green Cr means you owe them. A badge at the top shows total outstanding. Click any row to open and edit; Export saves the list to CSV.

Customers with transactions cannot be deleted Deleting them would break their bills and khata. This is intentional.

7. Making a Sales Bill

Press F2 — the screen you will use most. It is one page: customer at the top, items in the middle, money at the bottom.

Step 1 — the customer

Start typing a name in Customer. Matching customers appear; use ↓ and Enter, or click one. Mobile and area fill in automatically.

  • New customer standing at the counter? Press the + button beside the box. Enter name and mobile, press Save & use, and you are back on the bill with them selected.
  • Walk-in cash sale? Just type the name and carry on. Nothing else needed.

If the customer has an old balance, it appears in a coloured badge: Previous balance ₹9,500.00 Dr. Click View khata beside it to see their full account.

Bill settings

SeriesCOM for a normal bill, ESM for an estimate, Service for repairs
Bill No autoThe next number in that series
Bill DateToday by default
PaymentCash if paid now, Credit if on account

Step 2 — the items

There are three ways to put a piece on the bill.

MethodHow
Scan the barcode Click the Tag box and scan. Everything fills in instantly.
Type the tag Type RIN00002 in the Tag box and press Enter.
Search by name Type in the Item box. A list of matching pieces in stock appears showing tag, weights and purity. Choose one with ↓ and Enter.

However you choose it, the weights, purity, making rate and hallmark charge all come across from the tag. You only have to type the rate per gram.

For a stone or diamond piece, the grid has Stone Rate, Dia. Wt and Dia. Rate columns. The stone and diamond weight come out of the metal — they are not gold — and are charged separately at their own rate, shown as Stone Amount and Diamond Amount in the bill summary. If the piece was tagged with these rates, they fill in for you. For a plain gold piece, leave them blank and nothing changes.

9. Purchase and Metal Exchange

A simple cash purchase

  1. Press New Purchase.
  2. Choose the Supplier. Invoice number and date fill in.
  3. Choose the Metal — Gold, Silver or Platinum. It posts to that metal's stock and to the supplier's khata for that metal. (Refining, karagir issue/receive and cash settlement have the same dropdown.)
  4. Choose Cash or Credit.

  6. Fill in Discount, GST %, TCS % and Paid Amount as needed.

12. Gold Saving Scheme

Click Gold Scheme. This runs the monthly savings plans where a customer pays a fixed amount for several months and you add a bonus at the end.

Step 1 — create the plan (once)

  1. Open the Scheme Types tab and press New Scheme.
  2. Give it a name, for example 11 + 1 Gold Plan.
  3. Scheme Type — what the member is really saving up. See the table below.
  4. Period — whether instalments fall due in Days, Months or Years. The labels below change to match.
  5. Monthly Amount — what the member pays each month. On a Weight Wise plan this becomes Monthly Weight in grams instead.
  6. Paying Months — how many months they pay, for example 11.
  7. Total Months — paying months plus your bonus month, for example 12.
  8. Benefit at Maturity — your contribution at the end, in rupees, in grams, or as a making-charge waiver depending on the type.

A box shows the value at maturity as you type, so you can see exactly what you are promising:

₹2,000 × 11 months = ₹22,000 paid by the member + ₹2,000 benefit = ₹24,000 value at maturity

The four kinds of plan

Which weight plan should I offer? Both protect the customer from the gold rate rising. On Weight keeps the payment the same — ₹5,000 a month buys more grams when gold is cheap and fewer when it is dear. Weight Wise keeps the grams the same — one gram a month is always one gram, and the payment goes up and down instead. Customers who budget by the month prefer the first; customers saving for a particular piece prefer the second.

Step 2 — enrol a member

  1. Go to the Members tab and press Enrol Member.
  2. Choose the customer and the scheme. The amounts fill in from the plan.
  3. Set the start date and press Enrol.

The software immediately creates the whole instalment schedule — one row per month with its due date. The last row is your benefit, marked Shop benefit, which the member never pays.

Step 3 — collect each month

Open the member from the list. The schedule shows every instalment:

  • Green — received
  • Red — overdue
  • Plain — not due yet

Press Receive on the row being paid, confirm the amount, date and payment mode, and save. Pressed the wrong row? Press Undo and it reverses cleanly.

On a weight plan the box also asks for the rate per gram on the day of payment, and shows you the conversion before you save — ₹6,000 at ₹5,000/g — accrues 1.200 g. The schedule then carries a Weight column alongside the amount, so you can always see how many grams the member has built up and at what rate each instalment went in.

Reminders

Press Remind at the top to open WhatsApp with a message already written out — the member's name, the amount due, the due date and what they have paid so far. Check it and send.

Step 4 — spending the scheme on a bill

When the member comes to buy, make an ordinary sales bill as usual. Once you have chosen the customer, a Saving Scheme box appears in the bill's totals panel listing their open schemes. Pick one and the balance comes off the bill.

  • Scheme Amount — how much of the balance to spend. Leave it blank to spend as much as the bill can take.
  • On a weight plan you also enter today's rate per gram, which is what values their grams. The bill then shows how many grams were used.
  • Return in Cash — if the balance is bigger than the bill, the rest stays on the account for next time unless you type an amount here to hand back.
Scheme money comes off after GST, not before A discount reduces what you charge tax on. Scheme money is different — it is the customer's own deposit being handed back, so GST is charged on the full bill first and the scheme money then settles it, exactly like cash would:
Bill amount 52,000.00 + GST @ 3% 1,560.00 = Total 53,560.00 − GSS Amount 6,000.00 = To pay 47,560.00
An On Making plan is the exception: waiving making charges genuinely does reduce the bill, so that one goes in before GST and the tax falls with it.

Closing an account

When the scheme matures and the customer takes their jewellery, press Close Account. It stays in your records and can be seen under the Closed filter.

The balance always tells the truth A member's balance is worked out fresh from their receipts, less whatever bills have already spent. Undo a receipt, correct a bill or delete one and the balance simply re-reads correctly — there is nothing to reverse by hand and no way for it to drift. Your benefit is only added once all the paying instalments are in and the maturity date has passed, so a member who stops halfway gets back what they put in.
Scheme money is kept separate Money collected under a scheme is a deposit you are holding, not a sale. It is kept in its own account and deliberately does not appear in the customer's normal buying-and-selling khata, so neither figure confuses the other.

The tiles across the top give Pieces, Gross Weight, Net Weight, Fine Weight and Value at Cost. Fine weight is the number that matters — it is your actual pure gold, whatever purity the pieces are.

Value at Cost

This is what the stock cost you, not what it would sell for:

Value at Cost = Fine Weight × Cost per gram

The cost per gram comes from the Cost/Gm column you fill in when tagging (see Tag & Barcode). If you left it blank on some pieces, those pieces are left out of the total and the tile tells you how many were excluded — so a partial figure never reads as the full stock value. In the summary views an asterisk marks any group that contains uncosted pieces.

Cost is for your eyes only. It is never printed on a customer's bill.

Export saves whatever you are looking at as a CSV file for Excel.

Correcting a piece after a count

Found a piece that weighs differently to what was typed in, or a purity entered wrong? You do not have to delete the tag and start again.

  1. Switch to Tag-wise (detail) and leave the filter on In Stock.
  2. Press Edit Stock. The grid turns into boxes you can type in.
  3. Correct Gross Wt, Stone, Purity, Cost/Gm, Location or HUID on any rows you need. Changed rows turn green as you go.
  4. Press Save. The button shows how many pieces you have changed.

Net Wt and Fine Wt are worked out for you and update as you type — you never enter them by hand. Your metal on hand moves with the correction too, so the Day Book and the stock position stay right.

Sold pieces cannot be changed here A piece that has been sold is already priced on a customer's bill. Changing its weight on this screen would leave the bill and your stock telling two different stories, with nothing to say which one is right — so the software refuses, and tells you which tag. Open the bill and correct it there instead. If any row in a save is refused, none of them are saved, so you never end up half-corrected.

The Day Book

Click Day Book and pick a date. This is your end-of-day check.

The right-hand panel, Cash And Bank Accounts, shows for each metal what you started the day with and what you finished with, in three weights:

Gold :- Opening Closing Gross Wt 492.100 540.300 Net Wt 492.100 540.100 Final Wt 467.564 517.412

Use Gross Wt when you physically weigh your trays — that is the number a count produces. Net Wt takes the stones out, and Final Wt is the pure metal inside. Old gold taken from customers has its own block underneath, so it never gets mixed up with your own stock.

Below that, every cash and bank account with its opening and closing balance, then Today Received Details — what came in today, split by whether it was cash, card, UPI, NEFT or cheque, counting both bills and receipts.

Only cash goes in the cash drawer A card swipe, a UPI transfer, NEFT or a cheque all go to the bank, not the drawer — that is where the money actually lands. So if your drawer does not match the Cash Account figure at closing, something really is missing, and the number is worth trusting.

Branches and moving stock between them

Click Branches & Transfer. Use this if you have more than one shop, or a locker you keep stock in.

  1. On the Branches tab, add each shop or locker by name.
  2. On Transfers, press New Transfer.
  3. Choose the branch the pieces are leaving and the one they are going to.
  4. Scan each barcode, or press Add beside it in the list underneath.
  5. Press Save Transfer.

The top of the screen always shows where your stock is — pieces, weight and value at each branch. Got a transfer wrong? Delete it and every piece goes straight back where it came from.

A piece can only be in one place The software will not let you send a piece from a branch it is not actually at, or one that has already been sold — either would create stock out of thin air at the other end. If you rename a branch, everything recorded there comes with it.

If a piece has been moved on again since, you must delete the newer transfer first — otherwise undoing an old one would drag the piece backwards past a move that still stands. The software tells you which document is in the way.

Stock Verification — the physical count

Click Stock Verification. Use this to check that what is in your safe matches what the software says. Do it monthly at least.

  1. Take out your pieces tray by tray.
  2. Scan each barcode, or type the tag number and press Enter.
  3. Each piece you scan turns green and the Found counter goes up.
  4. When you have scanned everything, switch the filter to Missing.

Dashboard

Sales Register

Every bill for a chosen date range. Three tiles show number of bills, total sales and total outstanding. Each row has a print and a WhatsApp button. Click a row to open and edit the bill. Export gives you a CSV.

Returns — goods coming back

Open Returns from the sidebar. A return is a new document, not a change to the old bill: the original bill stays exactly as you printed it, and the return puts the stock, the money and the gold back on the day the goods actually came in.

Karagir job work — metal out and back

Go to Orders → Karagir Job Work and pick a goldsmith. This is where you keep him honest:

Outstanding — who owes whom, at a glance

Click Outstanding for two lists side by side: Debtors (everyone who owes the shop) and Creditors (everyone the shop owes), each with a running total. Two tiles at the top show your total receivable and total payable.

A Money (₹) / Metal (g) toggle reads the same thing two ways. In Money it is rupees from the khata. In Metal a dropdown picks Gold, Silver or Platinum and the lists show fine grams owed in that metal — never turned into a rupee value, because metal owed is metal and its price changes daily. Use this to chase payments, or to see at a glance how much gold your customers are holding against you. Exports to CSV.

Accounting Books — Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet

Click Accounting Books for the three statements your accountant asks for. Pick a date range at the top — it opens on the current financial year (1 April to 31 March); This FY resets it. Three tabs across the top switch between the books.

  • Trial Balance — every account with its Debit or Credit balance, in two columns that must add up to the same total. They always do; anything the books cannot otherwise place (usually your own capital brought forward) appears as its own line called Capital / Opening Difference.
  • Profit & Loss — a Trading account that works out your Gross Profit (sales plus closing stock, less purchases and old gold), then a Profit & Loss account that takes off shop expenses, karagir labour, refining and discounts to reach Net Profit. Two tiles at the bottom show both figures.
  • Balance Sheet — what the business owns (cash, bank, money customers owe, stock at cost) against what it owes (suppliers, GST, scheme deposits) and your capital. The two sides always match; capital carries the profit the period earned.
These books are built from your bills, purchases, receipts and payments — you do not post journal entries by hand. Two things to know: stock is valued at what you paid for it (the same Value at Cost as the Stock Report), taken from what is in stock right now; and gold is not mixed into the rupee books — metal has its own weight statement in the Ledger. Each book exports to CSV.

Cash Book & Registers

Click Cash Book & Registers for the day-to-day listing books. Three tabs across the top:

  • Cash / Bank Book — every receipt and payment through the account with a running balance, so you can tally the cash drawer or reconcile the bank. The dropdown switches between the Cash Book and the Bank Book.
  • Journal — every entry the software posted, in date order, with the account it touched and the debit or credit. This is the full audit trail behind the khatas.
  • Sales & Purchase Books — a register per document: Sales, Sales Return, Purchase, Purchase Return. Each lists the documents with their taxable value, GST and total, and totals the columns at the foot — handy at GST-return time.

Pick a date range at the top (it opens on the current financial year). Every view exports to CSV.

GST Reports

Click GST Reports at return time. Five tabs give you what the monthly return needs:

  • GSTR-1 (your sales) and GSTR-2 (your purchases) list every taxable bill and split the tax correctly — CGST + SGST when the customer is in your own state, IGST when they are in another — and mark each as B2B (a registered dealer, GSTIN on file) or B2C (a counter sale). Set your own state in Settings → Company so the split is right.
  • GSTR-3B is the summary: tax you collected on sales, less the credit for tax you paid on purchases, giving what you actually pay in cash (or the credit that carries to next month).
  • HSN Summary groups your sales by HSN code with the taxable value and tax — the annexure the return asks for.
  • TCS / TDS lists the TCS you collected on large sales and the TDS you withheld from karagir labour, so you know what to deposit.
The GSTR-3B figures are the same as the GST line in the Accounting Books — one number, two places, always in agreement. Everything here exports to CSV to hand to your accountant or upload to the portal.

16. Backup — Do This Every Day

This is the most important page in this manual All your data is in one file on this one computer. If the disk fails and you have no backup, every bill, customer and stock record is gone permanently. No one can recover it.

Taking a backup

  1. Go to Settings → Data & Backup.
  2. Press Create Backup.
  3. Choose where to save. The file is named with today's date automatically.
  4. The folder opens so you can confirm the file is there.

Where to keep backups

PlaceWhy
Pen driveKeep it away from the shop. Protects against fire and theft.
Google Drive or OneDrive folderCopies itself off-site automatically
Second computerProtects against one machine failing
A simple routine that works Take a backup to a pen drive every evening before locking up, and keep a second copy in a cloud folder once a week. Keep at least the last seven days — if a mistake goes unnoticed for a few days, you can still go back.

Restoring from a backup

Use this when the computer has been replaced, the data file has been damaged, or something has gone badly wrong and you want the books as they were. Only the owner can do it.

  1. Go to Settings → Data & Backup.
  2. Press Choose Backup File… and pick the .db file.
  3. The app reads the file and shows you what is inside it — shop name, how many customers, tagged pieces and bills, and the date of the last entry — next to the same figures for what is in the app right now. Nothing has changed yet.
  4. Check the two columns. If the backup is the one you want, press Replace my data.
  5. The app restarts by itself. Sign in again and check the Day Book.
Everything currently in the app is replaced. Any bills, receipts or stock entered since that backup was taken will no longer be there. If you are unsure, take a fresh backup first — then you have both.
You can undo a restore Just before replacing anything, the app saves your current books as a file named pre-restore-… .db in the data folder (the location is shown at the top of the same screen). If you restore the wrong file, restore that one to get straight back.

If the file you choose is not a backup, is from a different program, or has been damaged, the app refuses it and tells you why. Your books are not touched.

Backing up to Google Drive

The app can upload the backup to your own Google Drive with one press, or once a day by itself.

One-time setup

Google requires every program that touches Drive to be registered first. Doing this yourself means the backups sit in your Drive, under your control, and nobody else can reach them. It is free and takes about five minutes.

Go to Settings → Data & Backup → Google Drive Backup and press Show me how. The software walks you through all seven steps. In short:

  1. Open console.cloud.google.com and create a project.
  2. Enable the Google Drive API.
  3. Fill in the OAuth consent screen and set it to In production.
  4. Create an OAuth client ID of type Desktop app.
  5. Paste the Client ID and Secret into the app.
Set the consent screen to "In production" If it is left on "Testing", Google cuts the connection every seven days and you have to sign in again and again.

Connecting and backing up

  1. Press Connect Google Drive. Your browser opens Google's sign-in.
  2. Choose the account that should hold the backups and press Allow.
  3. Come back to the app — it now shows which account is connected.
  4. Press Back Up Now whenever you like, or tick Back up automatically once a day.

Backups go into a folder called Parivar Jewellery ERP Backups. The newest ten are kept and older ones removed, so your Drive does not fill up. Press Show backups in Drive to see what is there.

What the app can see in your Drive

  • It can only touch the backup files it created itself.
  • It cannot read, list or change anything else in your Drive.
  • Your Google password is never seen by this software — you type it on Google's own page.
  • You can cut off access any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or press Disconnect in the app.
Do not rely on one backup alone Google Drive is excellent, but keep a pen-drive copy too. Two places, not one.

Restoring

A backup is a complete copy of your data file. To restore it, close the software, copy the backup into the data folder shown on the Data & Backup screen, and rename it to parivar.db. If you are unsure, ask whoever supports your computers — do this carefully, as it replaces everything.

18. How Every Number Is Calculated

Nothing is hidden. Every figure the software shows comes from one of these formulas.

Weights

Net Weight = Gross Weight − Stone Weight − Black Beads Fine Weight = Net Weight × Purity ÷ 100 Example: 10.000 g gross, no stones, 91.6% purity Net = 10.000 g Fine = 10.000 × 91.6 ÷ 100 = 9.160 g

A sales bill

Goods Amount = Net Weight × Rate per gram Making Amount = Net Weight × Making per gram Bill Amount = Goods + Making + Hallmark charges Taxable = Bill Amount − Bill Discount − Making Discount GST = Taxable × GST % Total = Taxable + GST + Other charges + TCS Balance Due = Total − Old Gold value − Amount Received − GSS Amount Example: 12.000 g at ₹4,590, making ₹300 per gram, GST 3% Goods 12.000 × 4,590 = ₹55,080.00 Making 12.000 × 300 = ₹3,600.00 Bill = ₹58,680.00 GST 3% = ₹1,760.40 Total = ₹60,440.40

Old gold taken from a customer

Fine Weight = Net Weight × Purity ÷ 100 Value = Fine Weight × Rate per gram Example: 3.000 g at 80% purity, allowed at ₹4,500 Fine = 3.000 × 80 ÷ 100 = 2.400 g Value = 2.400 × 4,500 = ₹10,800.00

A purchase

Fine + Wastage = Net × Purity ÷ 100 × (1 + Wastage %) Amount = Fine + Wastage × Rate per gram Bill Amount = Purchase − Discount − Material Returned + GST + TCS

Refining

Fine Weight = Net Weight × Purity ÷ 100 Amount = Fine Weight × Rate per gram

Gold saving scheme

On Amount / On Making Value at Maturity = Monthly Amount × Paying Months + Shop Benefit On Weight (fixed money, grams follow the rate) Grams accrued = Amount Paid ÷ Rate on the day Balance = All grams accrued + Benefit grams Weight Wise (fixed grams, money follows the rate) Amount to pay = Monthly Weight × Rate on the day Balance = Monthly Weight × Paying Months + Benefit grams Spending it on a bill To pay = Total (incl. GST) − Old Gold − Received − GSS Amount Example: 6,000 paid when gold is 5,000/g = 1.200 g accrued the same 6,000 at 6,000/g = 1.000 g accrued

Balances

Money balance = Opening + (all debits − all credits) Gold balance = Opening + (metal given − metal received) Positive = Dr = they owe you Negative = Cr = you owe them

19. Problems and Solutions

What you seeWhat to do
"Tag … is not in stock — it cannot be billed again" That piece is already sold or has been melted. Check the tag number. The software is stopping you selling one physical piece twice.
"Tag … cannot be sent for refining" Same reason — the piece has already left your stock.
"A credit bill needs a customer" You marked the bill Credit but did not choose a customer. Either pick one, or switch the bill to Cash.
"Cannot delete: this item already has stock tags" Delete or sell the tagged pieces first.
"Cannot delete: this party already has transactions" Deleting them would break their bills and khata. Leave them in place.
"Invalid username or password" Check both, and that Caps Lock is off. Usernames ignore capitals but passwords do not. If it is still refused, ask the owner to reset it.
"This account is disabled" The owner has switched this login off. They can enable it under Settings → Users.
"This action is restricted to an authorised role" Your role may not do that — see the table in section 3. Ask the owner to do it, or to raise your role.
"Please sign in to continue" The session ended. Sign in again.
Nobody knows the owner password Any Owner login can reset another user's password. If every owner password is lost, the data file must be restored from a backup taken when it was known — another reason to keep backups.
"No mobile number on this record" Add a WhatsApp or mobile number for that customer under Customers.
The item search finds nothing Only pieces in stock are offered. If it is sold or melted it will not appear. Check the Stock Report.
Nothing appears in a report Check the date range at the top. Most reports open showing this month only.
Fine weight looks wrong Check the purity on the tag. A 22K piece entered at 75% will show far less fine weight than it should.
The customer's balance looks wrong Open their khata (Ledger) and read down the entries. Every figure has a document number beside it, so you can find the bill or receipt that caused it.
A bill was made by mistake Open it from the Sales Register and press Delete. The pieces go back into stock and the khata is reversed.
"Only … g of loose metal is available" You are trying to make pieces from more metal than you hold loose. Check the weights, or enter the purchase that brought the metal in. If the pieces were bought ready-made, use New stock mode instead.
Stock looks twice what it should Metal was probably purchased and then tagged using New stock instead of From loose metal, so it was counted twice. Delete those tags and re-enter them as a conversion.
Pasted rows do not appear The first column must be a gross weight. Check the column order shown above the grid, and that you copied cells rather than a picture.
Barcode labels print off-centre Choose the right label size in the print window, print one sheet on plain paper and compare, then adjust. Use Skip labels for a part-used sheet.
The scanner does not read a label Print larger — use the A4 sheet of 24 rather than 65 — and make sure the label is not creased across the bars.
Google Drive keeps asking me to reconnect The OAuth consent screen is still set to "Testing", which expires access every seven days. Set it to "In production" in the Google Cloud console.
"Could not reach Google" No internet, or a firewall is blocking it. Ordinary pen-drive backups still work offline.
Printing is misaligned Go to Settings → Invoice Design, check A4 or Thermal is right for your printer, and use Test Print until it lines up.

Golden rules

  • Give every person their own login, never share one, and sign out when you leave the counter.
  • Correct, do not duplicate. Edit the wrong bill rather than making a second one to cancel it.
  • Take a backup every day.
  • Verify stock monthly. Small differences found early are easy to explain.
